Puttanesca

A punchy, salty, deeply flavoured pasta sauce. Fancier ingredients make it better, but it's damn good with whatever you have on hand.

Ingredients — serves 4 - 6, 1 hour total

500gpasta (full packet)
2–3 tbspoil olive is best; sunflower or rapeseed fine. Butter needs mixing with a higher smoke-point oil
½ mediumonion, diced
3–5 clovesgarlic, sliced or minced
3–5anchovies, roughly chopped they melt away completely in the oil
5–10black olives, stoned and chopped salt- or air-cured preferred
~10pickled capers
2–3 tbspconcentrated tomato paste double-strength tube; reduce if using fresh tomatoes
1–3tomatoes, diced optional — reduce paste if using; cook until oil separates and turns red
a splashalcohol wine, beer, port, spirits — all fine
1–2 tspbalsamic vinegar
½ tspdried or fresh Mediterranean herbs
½ tspcracked black pepper
1 tbspsalt for the pasta water
0–1 tspsalt for the sauce taste first — anchovies and olives are already salty

Method

1
Sweat the onions
Heat the oil in a large pan over low heat. Add the onion and cover with a small lid or cartouche (foil pressed directly over the onions) to trap steam and speed up caramelisation. Let them slowly soften and turn golden — the longer you give them, the better the base.
2
Build the base
Add the garlic, anchovies, olives, and capers. Turn heat to medium-high. Let everything break down and colour — resist stirring constantly, leaving it briefly builds deeper flavour. Just don't let it burn.
3
Add tomato
Stir in the tomato paste, we want it to darken a little, a few minutes (and fresh/tinned tomatoes if using - longer like 10mins). Cook until the oil separates and turns a deep red. This takes patience but it's worth it.
4
Deglaze
theres still not much in the pan at this point. Splash in the alcohol and let it bubble and sizzle. Cook until the sharp smell has gone — the base should stay lively but not scorching.
5
Cook the pasta
Boil a full kettle. Salt the pot generously, add a splash of oil, then the pasta. Cook to just under al dente — it will finish in the sauce. Err on the side of undercooked.
6
Save the pasta water
Before draining, scoop out at least 2 cups of pasta water — more is better. Drain, then toss the pasta with a little oil so it stays glossy and doesn't clump. love your pasta!
7
Build the sauce
Add the pasta water to the base in gradual splashy bursts. The starch emulsifies with the oil into that silky, velvety texture. Keep at a gentle bubble, stirring so nothing sticks. you dont have to use it all ~ you want maybe < 3 cups of total sauce in the pan, by no means full ~ we still have to put the pasta in!
8
Season and finish
Add herbs, pepper, balsamic, and taste for salt. Let the sauce keep developing/evaporating until it coats a spoon nicely. finely chopped woody herbs (rosemary, thyme) can go in earlier during the dry stage with garlic etc instead.
9
Combine and rest
Turn off the heat, tip in the pasta, and fold everything together thoroughly. Let it rest a few minutes. If still too al dente, low heat with a lid — it keeps cooking gently.
10
Leftovers
Reheats brilliantly in an air fryer: a portion on foil, high heat, 15 minutes. A dash of soy sauce is an excellent optional move. Also great cold (not fridge-cold) with a squeeze of lemon or a splash of balsamic.

Notes: On ingredients: Use what you have. The anchovies, olives, and capers are all optional, as are the alcohol and balsamic and herbs and spices — any constrained pick of core ingredients works best ~ less is oftentimes more. The anchovies melt away completely, so don't skip them on principle. On salt: Anchovies, olives, and capers are all salty. Taste before adding any extra salt to the sauce. On tomatoes: If using fresh or tinned tomatoes instead of (or alongside) paste, cook them longer until the oil visibly separates and turns red. Reduce the paste accordingly. its quicker to get some blobs of concentrated tomato paste darkening in a hot pan and coloring the oil red than waiting for fresh/tinned tomatoes to evaporate n break down some until you can see the oil separate and begin to settle on top. On time: This sauce rewards patience. The longer the base cooks, the deeper the flavour. Even 20 extra minutes makes a real difference..
